Questions

The things people ask first.

How much does a website actually cost?

Most small business sites come in between ₹3,000 and ₹25,000 depending on how many pages you need and whether you need a database, bookings or payments. The ₹3,000, ₹6,000 and ₹9,000 packages are fixed prices, agreed in writing before any work starts, so the number never moves unless you ask for something extra. Payments and other custom work start at an estimated ₹25,000, and the quote is finalised once I know the requirements.

How long does it take?

Usually one to three weeks. The main thing that slows a project down is waiting for your text and photos, so once I have those, most sites are live within two weeks.

Do I own the website?

Yes. The domain, the hosting account, the code and any payment account are all registered in your name. If you ever want to work with someone else, you take everything with you — no permission needed from me.

Can you take card payments?

Yes. I set up Razorpay or PayU in your name, so customers can pay by UPI, card or netbanking — a deposit, a fixed price, or a small order. The gateway sends the receipt and confirmation email, and you can issue refunds from its own dashboard. It charges around 2% per transaction; that's their fee, not mine.

Can my staff log in to the site?

Yes, a staff login area can be added. It's quoted separately, because the work depends on what your staff need to see and do. We talk it through on the call.

I already have a website, but it's old. Can you redo it?

Often, yes — and it's usually cheaper than starting over, because we keep your existing text and photos. Send me the address and I'll tell you honestly whether it's worth rebuilding.

What happens after it goes live?

Thirty days of fixes are included. After that you can update the text and photos yourself — I'll show you how on the call. If you'd rather not touch it, the care plan is ₹399 a month: two changes, a backup before each one, and a monthly check that the site and the form are still working. Or manage it yourself; the site is yours either way.
